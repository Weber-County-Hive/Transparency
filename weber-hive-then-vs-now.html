Explain this simply

When a big project is first presented, people hear a number: so many homes, one hotel, a price.

This page lines up those first numbers with what the records show now for five projects.

Most of them grew. Some of the later changes were approved by staff, by landowners forming their own financing district, or by a vote that overrode the advisory board.

The costs of serving that growth, like fire trucks, roads and water, are shared by the wider public.

Public infrastructure district (PID)

A district created at a developer's request that can borrow for roads, water and sewer and repay it with a tax or assessment on property inside the district.

Development agreement

A contract between a county and a developer that sets what can be built, where, and on what terms.

Concept plan

A map inside a development agreement showing where lifts, roads, hotels and homes are planned in each area.

Slight and inconsequential

The standard in Powder Mountain's development agreement for changes county staff can approve without the County Commission.

Infrastructure financing district (IFD)

A district like a PID, created by landowner petition, that finances roads and utilities for new development.

Every large project in Ogden Valley starts with a public presentation: a number of homes, a hotel, a price tag. This case file lines up what four projects said when they were first presented with what the records show today, and the route each later change took.

Nordic Valley: 2022 vs. today

In April 2022, Utah Business reported the plan for a village at the base of Nordic Valley. By November 2024, when the County Commission created three public infrastructure districts for it, the plan had grown.

What was expected, and what has been authorized. When the PIDs were created, KSL reported the developers expected to levy about $47 million over 30 to 40 years, and the county's economic development director said the PID boards "won't create financial repercussions or debt obligation for the county." The first PID's March 31, 2026 financial report shows $39,380,000 in bonds payable. On April 15, 2026, its board authorized up to $25,000,000 more. Together that is up to about $64.4 million in principal, above the $47 million figure. The $25 million is a ceiling; whether all of it was issued wasn't found in the records reviewed.

Who pays for Nordic's fire truck?

The plan funds only "50%," or $1 million. No record reviewed says who pays the other half. If it comes from the fire district's regular budget, every property owner in the district pays, including Roy and Riverdale if the annexation passes. It's an open question. See the fire district's share.

Powder Mountain: 2008 vs. today

At a July 8, 2008 County Commission meeting on a proposed Powder Mountain rezone, under an earlier owner, the minutes record: "Currently, the existing zoning would allow 1,218 units." The proposed agreement "established the maximum density of 3,950 dwelling and hotel units." No action was taken that night. The current owner's development agreement with Weber County was recorded in 2015 and amended in 2019 and 2022; its unit total wasn't found in the records reviewed.

Eden Crossing

Eden Crossing is a mixed-use project at the New Eden crossroads, reported at up to 325 housing units, commercial space and a possible hotel on 20 acres. On Dec. 5, 2023, the County Commission approved its rezone 2–1, after the Ogden Valley Planning Commission had recommended denial 5–2. In September 2025, a county notice set a hearing on adding 8.73 more acres and amending the development agreement's "obligations, benefits, and standards." On Oct. 21, 2025, the County Commission approved the rezone of about 8.73 acres from AV-3 to form-based zoning and the agreement amendment, 3–0; the minutes say the applicant decided not to increase density. The state denied the project's water application in March 2025; a later exchange application, E6743, had a hearing July 14, 2026.

Longhorn and West Creek, West Weber

The same pattern shows up on the west side. On March 26, 2024, Weber County signed the Longhorn Estates development agreement, rezoning the land from agricultural A-2 to R1-15 single-family, with up to 116 lots. A March 17, 2026 county staff report says the 40-acre Longhorn Estates subdivision had preliminary approval for 84 lots, and that the agreement allows unused Longhorn density to be built on Burns's neighboring Meibos project instead: 22 of the 32 remaining lots would move there, bringing Meibos (about 64 acres, requested rezone A-2 to R1-15) to 208 lots and capping Longhorn at 94. Staff recommended approval; the final vote wasn't found in the records reviewed. The Longhorn Infrastructure Financing District, created in 2025, covers Longhorn parcels, a Meibos parcel and the West Creek parcel. It was formed on a petition by the landowners, who waived the statutory protest period; two of its three appointed trustees list Lync Construction email addresses. On Sept. 8, 2026, the Planning Commission's work session listed "West Creek Proposed R-3 Rezone and General Plan Amendment of 30 acres" on parcel 10-35-0072, one of the district's parcels, with Pat Burns. R-3 is a multi-family zone. A work session takes no action, and the outcome wasn't found in the records reviewed.

Snowbasin

In November 2022 the County Commission created the Mount Ogden Public Infrastructure Districts at Snowbasin's request, with up to $300 million in borrowing authority over 40 years. Its 2025 budget showed a $36,000 developer advance and no bonds issued. PID No. 1's 2026 budget, marked "PRELIMINARY DRAFT – SUBJECT TO REVISION" and dated Dec. 9, 2025, says "The district has no debt or leases" and budgets $30,000 in developer advances. That reflects the budget when it was prepared, not later borrowing, and covers PID No. 1 only.

What the records show

Each project's scope or financing grew after it was first presented. Several of the later changes went through routes with less public input than the first approval: an administrative finding, an owner petition with the protest period waived, or a vote over the advisory body's recommendation. The services that growth needs, including fire protection, roads and water, are paid for or shared by the wider public.

Grades

Graded on The Weber County Hive's five standing questions, from the point of view of the collective: everyone who pays for and lives with the result, not only the people nearest a project. Grades reflect what the public record shows, not the conduct of any person. Meeting a legal minimum earns a C, not a B.

The pattern across these projects

What the record shows was done, and what's still open

What the record shows was done

  • Each project's first approval had a public vote or hearing
  • Nordic Village's PID, project-area and bond documents are posted
  • The 2008 Powder Mountain commitments are in the county's minutes
  • County staff flagged the need to look at Powder Mountain Road

What the public doesn't have yet

  • Powder Mountain's unit total under its 2015 agreement
  • The outcome of the Sept. 8, 2026 Powder Mountain and West Creek items
  • Whether all of the $25 million in 2026 Nordic bonds was issued
  • How the other half of the Nordic ladder truck is funded
  • Cache County's approvals, which Weber County says it doesn't have

Questions readers are asking, and who can answer them

How big can Powder Mountain get without another vote?
Weber County's draft general plan says Snowbasin and Powder Mountain together hold 5,300 master-planned units that can be built without further legislative approval. The Powder Mountain share wasn't broken out in the records reviewed.
Ask the Weber County Planning Division: "How many units remain under Powder Mountain's development agreement, and how many have been built?"
Why did Nordic Village's bonds grow past the $47 million developers expected?
The records reviewed show the amounts, not the reasons.
Ask the Nordic Village PID board: "What is the total borrowed so far, and what is the most the district expects to borrow?"
Did the West Creek rezone go forward?
It was listed for a Sept. 8, 2026 work session, where no action is taken. No later action was found in the records reviewed.
Ask the Weber County Planning Division: "Has an application been filed for the West Creek R-3 rezone, and how does it relate to the Longhorn Estates development agreement's 116-lot cap?"
