No Property Tax, a New Line on the Power Bill: West Haven's 6% Energy Tax

West Haven has no city property tax. To cover rising costs without starting one, the City Council voted 3–2 to begin collecting a 6% tax on electricity and natural gas, the highest rate state law allows. The tax was enacted in 1998 and never collected. It starts Oct. 1, 2026.

Explain this simply

Most Utah cities pay for police, parks and staff partly with a city property tax. West Haven doesn't charge one.

Costs are going up, especially the city's contract with the Weber County Sheriff's Office for police service. So the council chose another way to raise money: a 6% tax on what people and businesses pay for electricity and natural gas.

The city passed this tax back in 1998 but never turned it on. In June, the council voted to start collecting it. Your power and gas companies will add it to your bill starting Oct. 1.

A property tax is paid by owners. This tax is paid by anyone with a power or gas bill in the city, including renters. That was the mayor's main reason for choosing it.

Municipal energy sales and use tax

A city tax on electricity and natural gas used in the city, charged as a percentage of the energy's delivered value and collected by the utility on each bill. Utah Code 10-1-304 allows up to 6%.

Delivered value

What the customer is charged for the energy delivered. The tax is figured on that amount, so a bigger bill means a bigger tax.

Ordinance 14-1998

The West Haven law, enacted May 20, 1998, that created the 6% energy tax. The city never collected it until now.

Resolution 20-2026

The council's June 17, 2026 resolution reaffirming Ordinance 14-1998 and authorizing the city manager to submit it to the Utah State Tax Commission so collection can begin.

General property tax

The tax a city charges on the value of homes, businesses and land. West Haven is one of the few Utah cities that doesn't charge one.

Truth in Taxation

Utah's notice-and-hearing process for raising property tax revenue. It applies to property tax, not to an energy tax, so it doesn't apply here.

Starting Thursday, Oct. 1, 2026, electricity and natural gas bills in West Haven will carry a new city tax of 6%. The West Haven City Council approved it June 17, 2026, by a 3–2 vote, as part of adopting the city's 2026–2027 budget.

The tax is not new on paper. The city's notice of public hearing states that West Haven enacted the Municipal Energy Sales and Use Tax on May 20, 1998, and that "it has not been implemented for the past 28 years." The council's action, Resolution 20-2026, reaffirmed that 1998 ordinance and authorized the city manager to submit it to the Utah State Tax Commission.

West Haven does not charge a city property tax. Council member Ryan Saunders said at the meeting that West Haven is "one of 3 cities that do not collect property tax," according to the minutes. Mayor Rob Vanderwood told KSL.com the energy tax was chosen to keep it that way: "We don't want our residents to be overburdened with taxes, so we're trying to keep the property tax out."

In one line

West Haven will collect 6% on electricity and natural gas starting Oct. 1, 2026. The city expects about $900,000 in the first nine months and about $1.2 million in a full year. The rate is the highest state law allows. The council split 3–2 after one member suggested 4%.

What changes on your bill

The hearing notice describes the tax as "a tax on every sale or use of taxable energy within the City, equal to 6% of the value of the taxable energy delivered to the consumer." In practice, that means Rocky Mountain Power and Enbridge Gas customers in West Haven will see the tax added to their bills. City Manager Shawn Warnke said the city's revenue estimate was based on figures the city requested from those two companies.

Why the city says it needs the money

Council members and the mayor gave these reasons on the record at the June 17 meeting, as summarized in the minutes:

WhoWhat the minutes record
Mayor Rob VanderwoodWalmart construction delays mean the city isn't yet receiving sales tax from that store. The city has no property tax, and because the energy tax "was already adopted it allowed for another option to be able to stay ahead of the possible deficits in the upcoming budgets." The council "has worked to slow the growth down and that has effected the budget."
Council member Ryan SwappThe Sheriff's contract has "a 7.6% increase this fiscal year and will increase by 17% next fiscal year," due to wages "that needed to be reassessed to address retention of employees." He supports 6% "because of increasing costs."
Council member Ryan SaundersPublic safety is "one of the largest costs within the budget." The Sheriff's contract increase and "the decrease from building" were "the largest deciding factor for him." He is "in favor of this tax but not a big fan of taxes in general."
City Manager Shawn WarnkeThe roughly $900,000 estimate covers three quarters of a year, and the tax would go into effect Oct. 1, 2026. A full year would be about $1.2 million.

Quotes are from the minutes, which summarize what was said rather than transcribe it.

The records reviewed for this case file don't show the budget's overall deficit projection or the dollar amount of the Sheriff's contract for 2026–27. The May 6 minutes list one payment to the Weber County Sheriff of $560,556 (invoice #75217).

What the public said

One resident spoke at the June 17 public hearing. The minutes record that David Foust:

  • "expressed concern about the energy tax being approved in 1998 and not being implemented until now"
  • "expressed concern about people in the community on a fixed income having to compensate for this new tax"
  • "argued that the new Walmart would allow for quite a bit of sales tax revenue"
  • "felt the city expanded faster than could be accounted for in long term infrastructure and that was the reason for the tax being implemented"

After the hearing closed, the mayor said that had the tax been collected since 1998, "it would have cost the citizens quite a bit of money," and that evaluating the tax "has been tough." Council member Dixon responded on growth that "property owners can sell their land at any point," that the Legislature "dictates what the City's can and cannot do," and that the city manages growth through zoning.

A second tax starts the same day

Oct. 1, 2026 is also the day Weber County's new 0.20% sales tax takes effect. In West Haven, the combined sales tax rate goes from 7.25% to 7.45%. West Haven's estimated share of the cities' transportation money from that tax is about $270,851 a year, according to Wasatch Front Regional Council estimates reported by KSL.com. That money is for transportation only; the energy tax goes to the city budget.

The two taxes are separate decisions by separate governments. See A Public Vote, but No Vote by the Public for the county's sales tax, and MAP 008 to see West Haven's rate next to every other Weber County city.

Questions readers are asking

When does it start?
Oct. 1, 2026, according to City Manager Shawn Warnke at the June 17 meeting and KSL.com's reporting.
I rent. Do I pay it?
Yes, if you have a power or gas bill in West Haven. The tax applies to energy used in the city, not to property ownership. The mayor told KSL.com that reaching renters as well as owners was part of why the council chose it.
Do businesses pay it?
The ordinance, as quoted in the city's notice, taxes "every sale or use of taxable energy within the City." The records reviewed don't break down how much of the revenue comes from businesses.
Is 6% the most a city can charge?
Yes. Utah Code 10-1-304 allows a city to levy the tax at "up to 6% of the delivered value of the taxable energy."
Why wasn't this collected since 1998?
The records reviewed don't say. The notice states only that the tax "has not been implemented for the past 28 years." The mayor said at the meeting that collecting it all those years "would have cost the citizens quite a bit of money."
Is this instead of a property tax?
That's how the mayor and council described it. West Haven has no city property tax, and council members said the energy tax lets the city cover costs without starting one. No Truth in Taxation increase for West Haven was found in the Hive's review of Weber County property tax increases since 2019.
Where does the money go?
Into the city budget. Council members pointed to public safety, and especially the Sheriff's contract, as the biggest rising cost. The records reviewed don't show the tax being set aside for any one purpose.
