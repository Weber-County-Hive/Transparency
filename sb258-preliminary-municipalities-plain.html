What is the law?

In 2024, Utah passed a law called S.B. 258. It lets up to three landowners ask the state to start a new town on their land. The county where the land sits does not get a formal say.

This kind of new town is called a "preliminary municipality." The state only takes two each year.

Supporters say it lets rural landowners plan their own future. Counties where it is being used say it skips their zoning rules, even though the new town will need county roads and services.

Who tried to use it?

In 2026, four landowner groups in Wasatch and Summit counties applied. That is twice the yearly limit.

Wasatch Highlands, east of Heber, was accepted. Bear Canyon was turned down because its land did not connect the right way. Lost Creek and Smooth Hollow were turned down because they came in after the limit was reached.

Lost Creek is an Ivory Homes project. Ivory Homes is the state's largest homebuilder.

The bill to change it

Rep. Tiara Auxier wrote H.B. 510. It would have given counties a role before a town application is filed. The House passed it 68 to 0.

Before the Senate vote, the bill was made weaker. Even then, it failed 14 to 12. Three senators were absent. It needed one more yes vote.

Where the checks come in

Ivory Homes had given campaign money to five senators before the vote. Four of them voted no: Ann Millner, Jerry Stevenson, Evan Vickers and Brady Brammer. A fifth, Lincoln Fillmore, was absent.

The checks were $500 to $1,000 each. Sen. Kirk Cullimore also got a check from Ivory Homes. He carried the bill in the Senate and voted yes.

Does this mean votes were bought?

No. The page does not say that. It shows the money and the votes side by side, so you can see them for yourself.

What's new

Wasatch County voted on Sept. 2 to ask lawmakers to repeal the law. Summit County and the town of Wallsburg asked for the same thing. Ivory Homes asked lawmakers to remove the two-a-year limit. The state has started working on Ivory's Lost Creek application again. Rep. Auxier plans to bring her bill back in 2027.
