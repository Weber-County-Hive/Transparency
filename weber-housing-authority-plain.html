What is this about?

The Weber Housing Authority is a public agency that helps people with housing. A board of trustees runs it.

Two of those trustees are married: Weber County Commissioner Gage Froerer and his wife, Gloria Froerer.

Where the money went

Weber County keeps a list of every payment it makes. From 2023 through 2025, those lists show the Housing Authority paid $19,623 to two companies with the Froerer name on them: RESCOM INC and Froerer Real Estate and Property Management.

It came up in 2019

When Froerer was put on the board in 2019, his two fellow commissioners said on the record that it was not a conflict of interest for him to serve alongside his wife. They did not say what rule they used to decide that.

What his form leaves out

Every year, Froerer signs a form that asks him to list the boards he sits on. The Housing Authority is not on his 2025 or 2026 form. Neither are two other boards he sits on: MIDA and Little Mountain Service Area.

Who checks these forms?

Only the official fills out the form. The county checks that it was turned in and filled out. No one checks whether the answers are true unless someone files a complaint.

There is also a box an official can check to hide their employer. The county said Froerer's employer was hidden because he checked that box. No proof was asked for.

The written penalty for not fixing a form after notice is a $100 fine and a misdemeanor. When a complaint was filed about Froerer's forms before, the result was an updated form and no public penalty.

Is this against the law?

This page does not say it is. Utah's disclosure forms are there so the public can see relationships like this and judge for themselves. They do not ban family businesses from working with public agencies.

What the records show is a pattern: board seats tied to real money that are missing from the one form made to show them.

What's new

Oct. 12, 2026: a county appeals board ordered Froerer's 2025 employer information released. His 2026 employer line stays hidden because he checked an "at-risk" box on the form.
