What is this about?

Every year, elected officials fill out a conflict-of-interest form. It tells voters who pays them and what businesses they are tied to.

Since May 7, 2025, the form has a new box. An official can check it if they, or their spouse, are an “at-risk government employee.” Then the county must black out where they work before the public sees it.

Who is “at-risk”?

The law names jobs like judges, peace officers and county attorneys. It also covers any government worker whose job, or a recent credible threat, would put them at real risk of harm.

County commissioners are not one of the named jobs.

The law says the county “shall redact.” Weber County's appeals board said that rule kicks in as soon as the box is checked. Its decision does not describe any check of whether the official qualifies.

At the Oct. 12, 2026 hearing, the county said it does not check whether an official qualifies, because the statute does not require it.

Elected, but not on the list

A commissioner holds a county job because voters elected him. That job is not on the at-risk list. The only way in is a catch-all that needs unusual work duties or recent credible threats, and the form does not ask which. So the official's own checkmark decides it, and Commissioner Harvey checked it too.

What happened in Weber County

The Hive's publisher asked for Commissioner Gage Froerer's forms without the black-outs. The county said no. She appealed.

On Oct. 12, 2026, the appeals board voted 3–0. Its Oct. 13 decision did two things:

  • 2026 form: stays blacked out, because he checked the box.
  • 2025 form: must be released. It was filed in January 2025, before the law existed. It lists Weber County and two real estate companies, Rescom, Inc. and Gage Froerer & Assoc. Inc.

The 2025 form had no box and was first posted in full. The county blacked it out later to match the 2026 form, and the board said it should not have.

What his forms listed over the years

  • 2018: Nordic Valley Land Associates, with no share shown.
  • Nov. 2022: a 16% family-trust interest, filed after a resident complained.
  • 2025: the company listed. Housing Authority seat not listed.
  • 2026: the company not listed, a week before it closed. Employer blacked out.

The $100 fine and class B misdemeanor are for not filing or not fixing a form after notice. Accuracy is looked at only after a written complaint. In 2022 that led to an updated form and no public penalty.

An old address in the law

The law sends readers to Section 63G-2-303 for the definition. In 2026 that section was renumbered. The board said the old section “no longer exists,” but the wording stayed the same, so it used the new section.

Is anyone breaking the rules?

This page does not say so. The law allows the box, and the board found the county had to honor it. What the records show is that voters see less, and the decision does not describe anyone checking who qualifies.
