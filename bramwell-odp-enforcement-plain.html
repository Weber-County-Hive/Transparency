What this is about

Utah has an office that helps government agencies protect your personal data. It is the Office of Data Privacy. Christopher Bramwell runs it. Gov. Spencer Cox named him the state's first Chief Privacy Officer in August 2021.

This case file does not accuse anyone of wrongdoing. It lays out what the office can do, in records and in his own words.

What the office does

The office writes a privacy framework, trains workers, gives advice and takes complaints. Its own list of duties does not include enforcement or audits.

If you have a complaint about an agency, you go to that agency first. If it is not fixed, an ombuds can help both sides talk it out. That is mediation, not a fine.

The law names the State Auditor and the Attorney General to enforce it. Both are elected separately.

Who watches the office

Bramwell reports to Marvin Dodge, head of the Department of Government Operations. Dodge also chairs a five-member board. One of that board's jobs is to look at how the privacy office is set up and what power it has.

A 2026 law, H.B. 450, moved support for a second group, the Utah Privacy Commission, from the State Auditor's office to the privacy office.

The report you can't read

Every agency must file a yearly report with the office. It lists the personal data the agency shares, sells or buys. That report is a protected record. The office can ask for it. The public cannot. The first reports are due by Dec. 31, 2026.

In his own words

In spring 2025, Bramwell answered eight questions in a public Facebook group. He wrote that his office "is NOT an enforcement mechanism." He said the state had no official policy yet on selling anonymized data. His personal opinion, he said, is that governments should not sell personal data.

Utah's privacy law for businesses has a fine of up to $7,500 for each violation. The law for government agencies does not appear to name a dollar amount.

Health records and breaches

Health records follow a federal law, HIPAA. Most Utah hospitals share records through a private nonprofit exchange. The state privacy office does not oversee it. You can opt out with a notarized form.

The University of Utah has had several data breaches, including four in 2023 from one software flaw.

Who can check

The State Auditor has a State Privacy Auditor. Those audits are public. A 2023 review of 1,617 agencies found most had not posted a privacy policy statement on their website.

What's new

In August 2026, Gov. Cox asked the privacy board and commission to review rules on license plate cameras. In September, a commission task force drafted ideas, such as a person checking every camera alert before police act.
