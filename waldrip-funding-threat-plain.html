What happened?

Steve Waldrip is Gov. Spencer Cox's senior adviser on housing. On Aug. 18, 2026, Heber City invited him to talk about the state's housing plan.

Partway through, he gave a different message. He said it came from the governor. He told the council that Wasatch County's state funding was "now at risk, meaning none."

Why did he say it?

He pointed to two county choices. First, the county said no to a plan for 144 lots on 740 acres of state trust land. Second, the county joined Summit County in asking the state to repeal S.B. 258. That 2024 law lets a few landowners ask the state to start a new town without county approval.

Why "wrong room"?

Heber City and Wasatch County are two separate governments. The city had no say in either county choice. The county heard the message from news reports.

Can road money be cut off like that?

The road money he named is called Class B and C money. It comes from state gas taxes. State law says it is divided among counties and cities by a set formula. Half is based on population. Half is based on road miles.

State rules let the transportation department, not the Governor's office, hold it back. That is for problems with how the money is used. Nothing in the rules lists a county's policy views as a reason.

County leaders met with the Governor's office on Aug. 28. Afterward the county said state funding would not be affected. The Governor's office did not say whether Cox told Waldrip to make the remarks. On Sept. 2, the county passed a resolution asking lawmakers to repeal S.B. 258.

Is this against the law?

This page does not say it is. It puts what was said next to what the law and records show, so you can judge for yourself.
