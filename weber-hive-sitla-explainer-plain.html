What is SITLA?

When Utah became a state, the federal government gave it land. Today a state agency called SITLA manages about 3.4 million acres of it. SITLA's job is to make money from that land for schools. The money is for public schools, universities and a few other named groups. It is not for the public in general.

Seven people sit on SITLA's board. The governor picks all seven, and the state Senate confirms them. One of them, Mike Mower, is also a senior adviser to Gov. Cox.

Can a county zone it?

A Utah law, §17-79-306, says county land-use law does not give a county power over land the state owns. That includes trust land.

In 2016, SITLA's top lawyer said counties have "no legal basis" for zoning state land. In the same talk he said that if a county says no, SITLA takes no for an answer. Both things are true at once. No Utah Supreme Court ruling has settled the question.

Has SITLA used its exemption here?

No public record we found shows that it has. A SITLA official said in 2024 it would. As of mid-October 2026, we found no report that it did.

What about the temple case?

People often mix this up with the Heber Valley Temple case. That case is about private land the church owned. It does not decide anything about SITLA. The Utah Supreme Court heard arguments on Sept. 4, 2026. It has not ruled yet.

Other counties

SITLA does not act the same way everywhere. In Wayne County in 2016, the county was told it had no zoning power and did not act. In Millard County in 2026, the county rezoned SITLA land through its normal process. In Wasatch County, SITLA has negotiated for about 20 years.

What's next

A new town called Wasatch Highlands is proposed next to the trust land. A public hearing is set for Oct. 27, 6 to 8 p.m., at the Wasatch High School auditorium.
