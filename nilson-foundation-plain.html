What happened

A new charity called the Nilson Foundation was formed in Utah on July 21, 2026. It is based in Ogden. Its president is homebuilder Jed Nilson. Mike Ostermiller is its treasurer and filed its paperwork. Anne Dunaway is its secretary.

Ten days later, on July 31, it posted a flyer on Facebook asking for furniture, beds and household goods. The flyer had a badge that said "501(c)(3) NONPROFIT." Another flyer said "ALL DONATIONS ARE Tax-Deductible."

What does 501(c)(3) mean?

It is the part of federal tax law for charities. A group is not a 501(c)(3) until the IRS sends it a letter saying so. That usually takes months. A group can apply with a form called Form 1023.

If it applies within 27 months of forming and the IRS says yes, the status reaches back to the day it formed. If the IRS says no, or the group never applies, it was never tax-exempt.

What the IRS search showed

The Hive searched the IRS's public list of tax-exempt groups on Aug. 31 and again on Sept. 3, 2026. Both times, there was no 501(c)(3) record for the Nilson Foundation.

That IRS list only shows approvals. It does not show applications still waiting. So we do not know if the group has applied.

In September, the group's own website still said "501(c)(3) Nonprofit — donations are tax-deductible."

Added Oct. 17, 2026: the Hive read the website's text again on Oct. 17. That wording was not found in the text. (Words inside pictures were not checked.)

What Utah law says

Since May 1, 2024, a new Utah charity can ask for gifts right away. There is no waiting period. That part is allowed.

The same 2024 law, H.B. 43, added a rule. It is a deceptive practice to falsely say a gift comes with a tax benefit. Its primary sponsor was Rep. A. Cory Maloy. Its Senate sponsor was Sen. Curtis Bramble.

Why it matters to donors

If a donor takes a tax deduction and the IRS later says no to the charity, the IRS can take back the deduction. It can add penalties and interest. It is up to the donor to keep track.

Wording that fits a group in this spot would say its application is pending and that gifts are not assured to be tax-deductible yet.

What we don't know yet

We have not confirmed who runs the group's Facebook page. We have not confirmed whether it has applied to the IRS. Nothing found so far shows it has asked for or received public money.
