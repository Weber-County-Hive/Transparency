How your tax bill works

Your property tax bill has a line for each group that taxes your home. That means the county, your school district, your city, and districts for fire, water, sewer and 911 dispatch.

When home values go up, Utah lowers each group's rate on its own. That keeps the money about the same as last year. To collect more, a group must mail a notice and hold a public hearing. That rule is called Truth in Taxation.

What we counted

We found 59 increases proposed for tax years 2020 through 2026. At least 46 were adopted. Some were made smaller before the vote. The 2019 notices were scanned images we could not read, so 2019 is mostly missing.

In 2022, all 13 were adopted. They included Harrisville (166%), Riverdale (94.8%), Weber Basin Water (62%) and both school districts.

When the state said no

In 2025, the Utah State Tax Commission blocked six Weber County increases. It pointed to tighter notice rules. The six were Roy, North Ogden, South Ogden, Harrisville, North View Fire and Central Weber Sewer. Their elected boards had voted yes.

2026 so far

Weber Fire District raised its tax 23.58%. Roy adopted 34.25%, cut back from 54.94%. Ogden Valley City approved 512.6%, but only if it wins a case at the Utah Supreme Court. Weber County did not raise its property tax. It chose a new sales tax instead.

Roy voters now get a say. A petition put Proposition 10 on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot. It asks whether to keep the increase or undo it.

Fire districts come up often

North View Fire proposed an increase every year from 2020 through 2026. Weber Fire District raised its tax for 2022 and 2026.

What this page does not say

These are the records we found. "Not found" never means "didn't happen." The notices give pay, staff, inflation, upgrades and new buildings as reasons. This page does not judge whether any increase was right.
