What is trust land?

When Utah became a state, it was given land to help pay for public schools. A state agency manages that land. It was long called SITLA. Its new name is the Utah Trust Lands Administration. Its job is to earn money from the land for schools.

What does the law say?

Counties and cities make zoning rules. Those rules say what can be built and how many homes fit on the land.

But a Utah law says state land is not under county or city zoning, unless another law says it is. That includes school trust land. It also covers a private builder who leases the land or has a permit to use it.

Who runs the agency?

A board of seven trustees runs it. The Governor appoints all seven. Six come from a list. The Governor picks the seventh on his own. The board hires the director, but only with the Governor's consent.

One trustee, Mike Mower, also works on Gov. Cox's senior staff. He has said he "represents the governor" on the board.

The land above Heber

The agency owns about 720 to 740 acres east of Heber in Wasatch County. In December 2024, an agency representative told the Heber City and Wasatch County councils the agency could use its exemption there. He said more homes could raise what the agency earns on the land.

So far, the agency and its builder have gone through the county's normal process instead. The county said no three times.

In August 2026, the county turned down a deal for 144 lots. Councilors pointed to density and water.

Other counties

It does not always go the same way. In Wayne County in 2016, the county stepped back after being told it had no zoning power. In Millard County in 2026, the county rezoned trust land the usual way.

What's new

In September 2026, the county manager said the county is open to other ideas, like a land swap for affordable housing somewhere else. We found no record that the agency has used the exemption on the Heber land.

Is this against the law?

This page does not say anyone broke a law. It shows what the law allows and what has happened so far.
