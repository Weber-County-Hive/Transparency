What is Amendment B?

Voters can put a law on the ballot themselves. That is called a citizen initiative. Right now it passes if more than half of voters say yes.

Amendment B would change the Utah Constitution. If a citizen initiative includes a tax change, it would need at least 60% of the vote.

Four kinds of tax changes count: a new tax, a tax on more things, a higher tax rate, or a property-tax rate that falls less than current law would.

What vote does it take?

Share of the vote needed to pass, by who is deciding.

Citizen tax initiative, today
50% + 1
Citizen tax initiative, if B passes
60%
Legislature passing a tax
50% + 1
Legislature putting an amendment on the ballot
two-thirds

What does it not change?

It does not change how the Legislature passes taxes. Lawmakers still need more than half of each house. It does not change other initiatives. Wildlife initiatives still need two-thirds. Local property-tax increases, PIDs, MIDA and UIPA are not affected.

If more than half of voters say yes on Nov. 3, 2026, it takes effect Nov. 23, 2026.

Two words matter most

"Includes" means the 60% rule applies if any of the four tax items is in the initiative, even if taxes go down overall. "Current law" is not defined, and the text does not say when it is measured. No court has decided how a tax swap would be treated.

Myths and facts (Part 1)

We checked 24 claims people made, from both sides. A few of them:

  • "B puts 60% on all initiatives." Myth. Only ones with the four tax items.
  • "We already need 60% for hunting." Myth. Wildlife initiatives need two-thirds, which is higher.
  • "Voting NO puts no new limit on the Legislature." Fact.
  • "If it doesn't work, it's easy to undo." Myth. It would be in the Constitution. Only the Legislature can propose a new amendment, with two-thirds of each house. Then voters decide.

History and related bills (Part 2)

In 2018 voters passed three initiatives. The Legislature replaced or rewrote all three. Medicaid expansion got 53.3% and medical cannabis got 52.7%. Neither reached 60%.

From 2022 to 2026 the state income-tax rate went down every year, from 4.95% to 4.45%. In the same years, other taxes went up or new ones were allowed. In 2026, 67 local taxing entities sought $93.58 million in property-tax increases. Weber County added a 0.20% sales tax by commission vote. B would not change any of those.

Who is involved?

The primary sponsor is Sen. Lincoln Fillmore. Rep. Jason Kyle is the House sponsor. The Utah Taxpayers Association supports it. Utahns Protecting Our Constitution opposes it and reported about $593,000 raised as of September 2026.

The official voter pamphlet has an argument for B by Fillmore and Kyle and an argument against by Sen. Luz Escamilla and Rep. Angela Romero. It says B likely would not change state revenue or spending in a material way.
