What is happening

Roy and Riverdale run their own fire departments today. Both cities asked to join Weber Fire District instead. The district would take over fire and ambulance service.

The district says this is not a new tax. It is not raising its rate. But Roy and Riverdale owners do not pay the district today. So its tax would be a new line on their bills, starting in 2027.

What the new line costs

The other half: your city line must drop

State law says each city must lower its own tax rate in the first year. The cut equals what the city budgeted for fire and ambulance service. That keeps people from paying twice.

Neither city has published its cut. So no one can say yet if an average owner pays more or less overall.

In both cities, fire costs more than the city's main property tax line. The records do not show how the cut will work when that happens.

After the first year

The required cut happens only once. After that, the city or the district can raise its rate. To do that, it must mail a notice and hold a public hearing first. Roy and Riverdale owners would also help pay for district costs already on the books, like new stations in Hooper and West Haven.

Why they say they're doing it

Leaders say one bigger department can share trucks and firefighters. The district's chief said service "should get better."

Roy's own tax increase

Roy's council also raised the city's own tax 34.25% in August. Voters will decide that increase on Nov. 3 as Proposition 10.

Who decides after

Today, each city council sets its fire budget. After joining, the district's board would set the rate and budget. The board has 11 members. The records do not say yet whether Roy and Riverdale each get a seat.

What's new

The hearings were set for Oct. 1 at Station 61 in Farr West. The district found that mailed notices for the July hearings went out late, for both cities, so it held the hearings again. Riverdale's staff said the change would save the city about $1 million a year. No minutes from Oct. 1 were found online yet.

How to take part

Owners and registered voters can send a written protest within 30 days after the Oct. 1 hearing. By our count that is Oct. 31. Enough protests force an election. Email district@weberfiredistrict.gov or call 801-782-3580.

Power C. Transparency C. Financial accountability D. Community impact C−. Hearings and notices were held. The net numbers have not been published.
