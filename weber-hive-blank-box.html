WASATCH COUNTY · DISCLOSURE RECORDS

The Blank Box: Three Disclosure Forms, One Pattern

Three officials in Wasatch County sit on overlapping nonprofit boards. Three separate conflict-of-interest disclosure forms, filed for three separate offices, each ask the same basic question — and each comes back with the same answer.

The Weber County Hive · Records review

CASE 2026-COI-01 · Posted Aug. 30, 2026 · Updated Oct. 17, 2026

New to this? Read it in plain words About 500 words, with simple charts.
Short version
  • Three Wasatch County officials filed conflict-of-interest forms in 2026 for three different offices.
  • Two forms checked "no conflict" with every line blank. The third named one board.
  • State corporate filings and other public records show board seats and businesses that are not on those forms: at least 5 for Erik Rowland, 4 for Dallin Koecher and 3 for Rachel Kahler.
  • All three are tied to the same downtown nonprofit, the Community Alliance for Main Street (CAMS).
  • This page compares what the forms say with what the records show. It does not say any law was broken.

How to read this

  1. Utah county officials and board members are required to file a conflict-of-interest disclosure — a short form asking what other boards they sit on, what businesses they own, and where they work.
  2. State corporate filings, contract records, and each entity's own paperwork are separate, independent public records — they don't rely on what any one official chooses to write down.
  3. Below, each official's actual disclosure form is compared against what those independent records show. The gap between the two is the story.

Three officials, three forms

Erik Rowland Wasatch County Council, Seat C · MIDA board ▸

Form filed

Wasatch County Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form, submitted as his MIDA board disclosure, dated Jan. 2, 2026.

☐ I have no conflict of interest to report

All three disclosure lines left blank — including no mention of a spouse, despite the form's own instructions asking filers to list boards "you (and your spouse) sit on."

Confirmed but not on the form

  • CAMS board seatCommunity Alliance for Main Street, Heber City nonprofit
  • JASE Medical / SignalRx, LLCOwner/COO per his own LinkedIn; Member per state LLC filing
  • TimeMD, incOwner, per his own LinkedIn, active since 2006
  • Rowland Development LLCSole Member, formed 2022 while already on County Council
  • Triumph Assurance Group LLCManager, formed 2024, shared address with SignalRx

MIDA's own statutory disclosure form — required under Utah Code §63H-1-304 — asks explicitly for "each entity or organization… in which the individual currently serves… in a paid or unpaid position on a board of directors." Rowland did not file that form; he filed the generic county form instead.

Source: Rowland's Jan. 2, 2026 disclosure form; Utah Division of Corporations filings; LinkedIn.
Dallin Koecher Wasatch Open Lands Board ▸

Form filed

Annual Wasatch Open Lands Board Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form, signed Feb. 4, 2026, notarized.

Listed: Heber Valley Chamber of Commerce — Director

That's the only entity named. The lines asking about personal conflicts and other business relationships were left blank.

Confirmed but not on the form

  • CAMS board seatDirector since 2019
  • Wasatch County Arts CouncilDirector and original incorporator since 2016; also the entity's registered agent
  • Heber Valley MusicDirector and trustee since 2019
  • Red White and Blue Festival Inc.Director

Three of those four entities — CAMS, Wasatch County Arts Council, and Red White and Blue Festival Inc. — are all registered at the same address: 475 N Main Street, Heber City.

Source: Koecher's Feb. 4, 2026 WOLB disclosure form; Utah Division of Corporations filings.
Rachel Kahler Wasatch County Council, Seat A candidate ▸

Form filed

Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form, bundled into her Declaration of Candidacy, filed January 2026.

☐ I have no conflict of interest to report

All three disclosure lines left blank. Notably, Kahler filed proper disclosures for her original 2019 Heber City Council run — this is a break from her own prior record, not a first-time omission.

Confirmed but not on the form

  • CAMS Executive DirectorPaid position, roughly $40,000/year, three-year Heber City contractAdded Oct. 17, 2026: the Park Record reported the contract at $39,900 a year, no benefits (April 24, 2026).
  • Red White and Blue Festival Inc.Director, on a board shared with Matt Brower and Dallin Koecher
  • 5 In The Hive, LLCSole Manager and registered agent. Stated purpose, per its Certificate of Organization: "to acquire, own, sell, and otherwise manage real estate investments"

Her campaign financial disclosures — a separate filing requirement — are on record and up to date. It is specifically the conflict-of-interest disclosure that shows this gap.

Source: Kahler's Declaration of Candidacy / COI disclosure; Heber City contract records; Utah Division of Corporations filings.

Where the threads connect

One address, three nonprofits

475 N Main Street, Heber City is the registered address for CAMS, Red White and Blue Festival Inc., and Wasatch County Arts Council — all three tied to Koecher in some capacity.

A shared board

Kahler, Matt Brower, and Dallin Koecher all sit together on Red White and Blue Festival Inc.'s board — the same three names tangled in the CAMS disclosure questions.

CAMS's state registration status: Delinquent (when checked)

The nonprofit's own state corporate registration showed a status of "Delinquent" when it was checked for this page — separate from any individual board member's disclosure gap. On Sept. 3, 2026, the Hive's Matt Brower page recorded the status as "Active, but Delinquent." The status may have changed since; the Utah Division of Corporations business search shows the current status.

A disqualification precedent

CAMS board chair Tom Stone was disqualified from a 2024 Wasatch County School Board race for missing a financial disclosure deadline, then sued the county clerk over it.

How these forms are checked

The official is the only person who fills out the form. A filing office receives it, but no office is required to compare the answers with corporate filings or other records when it is turned in. Questions about accuracy are usually examined only after someone files a complaint. The Hive's Weber Housing Authority page walks through the state law on this, and Utah's Campaign-Finance Enforcement Gap shows how earlier complaints ended.

That is why this page compares each form with independent records. Those records are public, and anyone can check them.
