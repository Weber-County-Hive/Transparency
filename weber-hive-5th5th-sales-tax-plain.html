What happened

Weber County has a new sales tax. It started Oct. 1, 2026. It adds 20 cents to every $100 you spend on taxable things. In most of the county, the rate went from 7.25% to 7.45%.

The three county commissioners voted yes. The vote was in a public meeting. It is expected to bring in about $13.1 million a year. Part of it will be paid by visitors and shoppers from outside the county.

What voters did not get

The law let the county put a question on the ballot. It did not have to, and it did not. A property tax increase would have meant a letter to every owner and a public hearing. This tax did not need either one.

Where the money goes

The $13.1 million a year, split three ways

From the Wasatch Front Regional Council's estimate.

Cities: roads only
$3.27 million
County: roads or public safety
$3.27 million
County: any use until Sept. 2029
$6.54 million

The biggest piece, $6.54 million a year, is the county's to spend as it chooses until Sept. 30, 2029. A commissioner said the money would "more than likely" go mostly to the sheriff's office. In 2023, voters said no to a $98 million jail bond.

Money promised before it arrives

New homes and businesses add to the tax base. In some areas, a share of that new tax money is promised to a project first. The county, schools, fire and water get less of it, sometimes for decades.

Who pays

A Public Infrastructure District, or PID, borrows money for a new subdivision's roads and pipes. The people who buy homes there later pay it back on their tax bills. The State Auditor said Utah PIDs had issued about $5.267 billion in bonds as of Aug. 7, 2026.

A 2026 bill to require more PID disclosure, H.B. 422, passed the House 66–1. It did not pass the Senate. Another law, H.B. 507, requires buyers to be told about PID charges "at or before closing."

Many other local governments in Weber County did raise property taxes, with letters and hearings. The county's own 2025 budget plan showed several funds drawing down, including about $15.9 million in its transportation fund.

Is this against the law?

The record shows the tax was adopted legally, in public. The question the case file asks is different: should people get a dollar estimate for their own household, the way they do for a property tax increase?
