Every Weber County Property Tax Increase Since 2019

The county, the school districts, the cities, and the fire, water, sewer and dispatch districts: every Truth in Taxation increase found in the public record for tax years 2019 through 2026, with what it was for and where the numbers come from.

The Weber County Hive · Independent Accountability Journalism
The short version
  1. 59 increases were proposed by Weber County taxing entities for tax years 2020 through 2026 in the records reviewed. At least 33 were adopted.
  2. 2022 was the peak: 13 increases, every one adopted, including Harrisville (166%), Riverdale (94.8%), Weber Basin Water (62%) and both school districts.
  3. Both fire districts raised taxes repeatedly. North View Fire proposed an increase every year from 2020 through 2026; Weber Fire District raised its tax for 2022 and 2026 and proposed an increase for 2023.
  4. In 2025, the state stopped six of them. The Utah State Tax Commission denied increases from Roy, North Ogden, South Ogden, Harrisville, North View Fire and Central Weber Sewer, citing new notice rules.
  5. In 2026: Weber Fire District 23.58%, Roy 34.25% and Ogden Valley City's conditional 512.6%. Weber County skipped a property tax increase and adopted a new sales tax instead.

Counts are from the records reviewed and will change as more minutes are checked. 2019 notices were scanned images that couldn't be read, so 2019 is mostly missing. "Not found" never means "didn't happen."

Explain this simply

Your property tax bill has a line for each group that taxes your home: the county, your school district, your city, and districts for fire, water, sewer and 911 dispatch.

When home values go up, Utah automatically lowers each group's rate so it collects about the same money as the year before. That's called the certified rate.

To collect more than that, a group has to mail a notice and hold a public hearing. That's called Truth in Taxation. This page lists every one of those increases found in Weber County since 2019.

2025: when the state said no

On Oct. 2, 2025, the Utah State Tax Commission denied property tax increases from 35 entities statewide, KSL reported, citing notice requirements tightened by S.B. 29 (2024) and S.B. 202 (2025). Six were in Weber County. Their elected boards had voted for the increases; the state blocked them.

What else changed in the same years

While these increases went through, new tools were added that send part of the future tax money from new growth to specific projects instead of these entities' budgets.

  • 2019
    S.B. 228 makes public infrastructure districts (PIDs) legal in Utah.
  • Nov. 2022
    Mount Ogden PIDs created for Snowbasin.
  • 2023
    Promontory Commerce Center PIDs created. Chromalox CRA hearing (county, school and fire districts later agreed to share increment).
  • May 2024
    Utah Inland Port Authority adopts its West Weber project area, about 8,968 acres.
  • Nov.–Dec. 2024
    Nordic Village PIDs and CRA; the county shares 75% of its increment there, and the school, fire and water districts 50%, for 15 years.
  • Aug. 2025
    Weber Fire District approves its Farr West Landing CRA agreement.
  • Oct. 1, 2026
    County's new 0.20% sales tax takes effect.

The notices reviewed give pay, staffing, inflation, required upgrades and new buildings as the reasons for these increases. None ties an increase to tax-increment sharing. Sharing reduces the growth money these entities would otherwise have to meet rising costs; for most of these projects it has only just begun or starts in 2027.

No increase found

No Truth in Taxation increase was found in the records reviewed for Plain City, Farr West, Marriott-Slaterville, West Haven, Huntsville, Weber Mosquito Abatement, Bona Vista Water or Uintah Highlands. Weber County skipped increases for tax years 2025 and 2026. That can change as more records are checked.

How this was compiled, and its limits
  • Sources: Weber County's combined notices of proposed tax increases (2021, 2025, 2026 read; 2019, 2020 and 2022 were scanned images that couldn't be read), the Utah Taxpayers Association's 2024 Truth in Taxation list, entity minutes and notices on the Utah Public Notice Website, and reporting by the Standard-Examiner, KSL, Deseret News and FOX13. Each row links to its own source.
  • Documents were read through text extraction or web-page text, not rendered pages.
  • Percentages for the same increase often differ slightly between the county notice, the entity's notice and the Taxpayers Association list; the page shows "about" where they differ. 2020 news reports gave dollar changes, not Truth in Taxation percentages, so 2020 shows dollars.
  • "Proposed" means the notice was found but a record of the final vote wasn't. Adoption for those rows still needs to be checked in each entity's minutes.
  • Harrisville's 2026 notice is left out of the counts: a meeting summary indicates the council adopted the certified rate, which would mean no increase. It hasn't been confirmed in the city's minutes.
  • North Ogden proposed about $150,000 for 2026 and dropped it in June 2026; it isn't counted.

Public records only. No comment requested.
