Who is he?

Matt Brower runs Heber City day to day. His title is City Manager. The City Council hires him. Voters do not elect him. He has had the job since 2018.

What is CAMS?

CAMS is short for the Community Alliance for Main Street. It is a nonprofit that runs downtown events in Heber, like the Heber Market on Main. Brower has sat on its board since 2019. Board members are volunteers.

The contract

Heber City agreed to pay CAMS $39,900 a year for three years. That is nearly $120,000 in all.

CAMS's board approved the deal first, on Jan. 13, 2026. Brower is on that board. A week later the City Council approved it, 5 to 0. The staff report that went to the Council was under Brower's name. The minutes say no conflicts were disclosed.

Who signed?

The contract says the Mayor signs for the City. But the signature page has no line for the Mayor. Only one person signed for the City: Brower.

What happened next

Someone sent a complaint to the Utah State Auditor. The office confirmed it is looking into it. On April 21, 2026, the City Council approved the contract a second time, 3 to 2, as a "sole source" deal.

Hundreds of residents signed a petition asking the Council not to renew Brower's own contract. The Council renewed it anyway, 4 to 2, on April 14. Not everyone opposed him. At least one resident wrote in support.

Where CAMS stands with the state

When we checked on Sept. 3, 2026, the state business registry listed CAMS as "Active, but Delinquent." That is the state's own label for a filing status. It may have changed since.

Is this against the law?

This page does not say it is. It puts the records side by side so you can see them for yourself. Some claims made in public comments are still unconfirmed.
