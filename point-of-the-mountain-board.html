Case File · Point of the Mountain State Land Authority

Sponsor and Co-Chair

For three sessions in a row, the House sponsor of the bills that reshaped the Point of the Mountain State Land Authority was Rep. Jordan Teuscher, who co-chairs that authority's board. The Senate sponsor, Sen. Jerry Stevenson, sits on the same board. Here is what the bills, votes and board minutes show.

The short version
  • The Point of the Mountain State Land Authority runs the redevelopment of the old state prison land in Draper, known as The Point.
  • Rep. Jordan Teuscher has co-chaired its board since January 2023, when the House Speaker named him. Sen. Jerry Stevenson is also a board member.
  • In January 2024 the board, with both men there, voted to support changes to its own law. Weeks later the two sponsored S.B. 198, making changes of that kind.
  • Stevenson was the Senate sponsor and Teuscher the House sponsor of S.B. 198 (2024), S.B. 166 (2025) and S.B. 278 (2026), all changing the authority's law. All three passed and were signed.
  • S.B. 166 rewrote how the board is made up, including the seat the House Speaker fills and how its co-chair is picked.
  • After S.B. 278 passed, Teuscher presented it to the board, then moved the board's vote to change nine public infrastructure districts to comply with it. The minutes record no conflict statement.
  • Stevenson's 2026 conflict-of-interest form lists his seat on the board. None of Teuscher's five forms filed 2022–2026 names the authority.
  • This page lays out what the records show. It does not say any law was broken. Legislators serving on state boards is allowed, and these bills were public and voted on.

The board

The Legislature created the authority in 2018. Its job is to plan and build on the state land where the old Utah State Prison stood in Draper. Its board decides how the land is sold, leased and built on.

The state's public meeting website lists these board members today: Jordan Teuscher, Lowry Snow, Jerry Stevenson, Jefferson Moss, Dawn Ramsey, Troy Walker, Jenny Wilson and Mike Ambre (the authority's executive director).

Co-chair since January 2023: The Jan. 10, 2023 board minutes say the House Speaker named Reps. Jeff Stenquist and Jordan Teuscher to the board, "with the latter assuming the role of co-chair." A May 2023 press release from The Point quoted "Utah State Representative Jordan Teuscher, co-chair of the Land Authority." Board minutes from Oct. 8, 2024 through 2026 list "Rep Jordan Teuscher, Chair" next to Lowry Snow.

At the board table

Jan. 8, 2024 (before the session). Teuscher chaired this meeting; Stevenson was present. Under "Proposed Legislation for the 2024 Session," Stevenson and the authority's director reviewed planned changes to the authority's law. The list included making clear that "board members may lease property from developers on the same terms as anyone else," adding newly bought land to the authority's land, giving the authority state-agency services, and sending it part of the state sales tax from The Point. The board voted unanimously to support the changes. Weeks later, Stevenson and Teuscher sponsored S.B. 198 (2024), whose summary lists the same kinds of changes, including "limitations on Authority board members."

At the same meeting, the board also voted unanimously to drop a rule that required keeping a record of meetings with interested parties, "reflecting the sensitive nature of real estate negotiations." The gift rule stayed the same.

March 11, 2026. Under "Legislative Updates," the minutes list Rep. Teuscher as presenter. He reported on S.B. 278 (his bill: 50 acres for for-sale housing, authority appointments to public infrastructure district boards, privilege tax changes) and on two bills he did not sponsor: H.B. 597 (alcohol rules, sponsored by Rep. Jefferson Burton with Sen. Stevenson) and H.B. 475 (economic development coordination, sponsored by Rep. Calvin Roberts and Sen. Kirk Cullimore). No vote on the bills was taken. He seconded two routine motions (minutes and financial report).

June 10, 2026. Co-Chair Teuscher presented amendments to the governing documents of public infrastructure districts 1 through 9 "to comply with Senate Bill 278," adding authority representatives to those districts' boards. He moved to approve them and authorize the co-chairs to sign Resolution 2026-05. Mayor Walker seconded. The vote was unanimous.

What the minutes don't show: neither set of minutes records a conflict-of-interest statement or a recusal by Teuscher. The minutes also do not say one was required. The June 10 attendance list does not match the body of the minutes (it lists Walker and Stevenson absent, but Walker seconded a motion and Stevenson moved one), so the minutes may contain errors.

How these were read: the board minutes are PDF files on the Utah Public Notice Website, read Nov. 26, 2026 using a page-reading tool that summarizes the text; the exact wording quoted here is limited to short phrases.

The Legislature's own review

On Oct. 21, 2026, the Economic Development and Workforce Services Interim Committee heard a staff review of the authority as part of a study of "whether and how" development authorities like MIDA, the Inland Port and The Point meet their purposes set in law. The staff brief lists the seven-member board as set by S.B. 166. The brief does not discuss board members who sponsor the authority's bills.

What the law allows

Utah law lets legislators serve on state boards, and for years the authority's own law required the House Speaker to appoint House members to this board. After S.B. 166, the Speaker's seat goes to someone "with relevant business expertise"; the law does not bar a legislator from holding it.

Legislators also file a yearly conflict-of-interest form. Item 5A asks for any "paid or unpaid position on a board of directors."

What could have been done differently: a short statement on the bill floor, and at the board table, noting the sponsor's board seat; a roll-call vote on floor substitutes; and the board seat named in the bill descriptions on his campaign site.

Three ways to look at it

An auditor's look: The bills, votes and minutes are public. The June 10, 2026 minutes list two members as absent who are also shown making and seconding motions. So the minutes may need fixing. No conflict statement shows up in the two meetings we read.

The Legislature's look: Utah law lets lawmakers sit on state boards. For years, this board's law required House members on it. Sponsors who sit on a board often know its needs best. The question is whether the public is told.

The collective look: The land, its tax money and its building districts belong to the public. Readers can see the votes. Stevenson's 2026 form lists his board seat. Teuscher's lists none. The bill descriptions and minutes don't mention it.

What could have been done differently: a short statement about the board seat on the floor and at the board table, and roll-call votes when a bill is swapped for a new version.
