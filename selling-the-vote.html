The short version

  • Utah campaigns are sold like products: mail, TV, digital ads, text messages, polls and paid signature gatherers. Most of it is legal, and much of it is reported. The gaps are in who paid first and who did the work.
  • Super PACs spent about $3 million on Utah network TV ads in the 2026 general election, according to FOX 13. In 2024, Republicans and their allies accounted for 98% of Utah's political ad spending (Axios, AdImpact data).
  • The Prop 4 repeal paid a Wyoming firm more than $4.3 million for signature gathering. Utah County's clerk referred 27 packets to the county attorney for possible fraud; no charges were reported.
  • The committee against Amendment B reported spending $3,142,705.81 in 2026 through Oct. 22, most of it in the last four weeks: about $2.09 million to one ad firm, Viewpoint Media (2026 General report).
  • Bills get sold too. The Hive has graded bills whose title promised one thing while the final law did another.

Mail: the party's postage permit

The Utah Republican Party lists "postage discounts for mailers" as a resource for its candidates. The Hive's case file on it is in Unelected Power.

  • The rule: USPS Publication 417 lets a state party committee mail at nonprofit prices. The committee "must have final authority over the decision to mail." Mail paid for with money a candidate "specifically earmarked" for it is "not eligible for the nonprofit prices" (USPS Pub. 417, 5-4.2; USPS ruling PS-055).
  • What was reported: a 2025 Provo mayoral postcard read "Postage Paid by the Utah Republican Party," and the campaign reported paying the party just under $1,400 for postage plus a small donation. The state elections director said parties may pay for mailings but must disclose it (KUER, 10/23/2025).
  • The Hive's findings: 2026 records show candidate money going in and party postage going out in the same weeks (The State Party Indicia, UP 100.13).

Signatures: a paid industry

  • How gatherers are paid: since 2021, Utah bans paying gatherers per signature. They must be paid by the hour and wear a badge naming who pays them; breaking the rule is a class B misdemeanor (H.B. 136, 2021). In 2026 the same rules were extended to people collecting signature removals (H.B. 242, 2026).
  • The Prop 4 repeal: Patriot Grassroots (Wyoming) was paid more than $4.3 million. Utah County Clerk Aaron Davidson referred 27 packets to the county attorney, calling it "the most extensive fraud that we've seen." GOP chair Rob Axson said the workers involved were fired and the packets flagged, and the firm's contract was ended. No charges were reported (Democracy Docket, 2/9/2026). Wasatch and Summit clerks reported finding no fraud in their packets (KPCW).
  • The removal campaign: more than 2,300 removal requests statewide by Feb. 12; Salt Lake County had more than 1,200, compared with 12 in the 2025 union-law referendum (KSL, 2/12/2026).
  • The "warning" text: a text paid for by the Republican National Committee and authorized by Utahns for Representative Government warned voters about canvassers working for "a firm under criminal investigation in another state." The firm's president called the claim "a lie"; the Missouri investigation KSL found was civil (KSL, 2/17/2026).

Polls and "message testing"

  • The law: a pollster must "disclose to the person being surveyed who paid for the poll"; the Lt. Governor imposes a $100 fine (Utah Code 20A-11-905, from H.B. 44, 2013). Text read from FindLaw; check the current code before quoting.
  • A 2020 example: a governor's campaign paid $88,576 for voter calls that respondents weren't told it paid for. The campaign called them message testing, not push polls (Utah Investigative Journalism Project, 10/18/2020).
  • Public polls: the Deseret News/Hinckley polls are conducted by Morning Consult. Ballot-measure campaigns commission their own: Better Boundaries' 64% poll was by Embold Research (online, 1,731 voters). Compare who paid before comparing numbers.

"Paid for by"

  • Utah requires "paid for by" wording on ads, including direct mail, naming who paid and whether a candidate authorized it; small items like yard signs are exempt (Utah Code 20A-11-901; current text to be checked on le.utah.gov).
  • In 2026 about 6,000 handwritten postcards against Senate President Adams carried no "paid for" line; volunteers bought their own postage. His campaign filed a complaint (KSL, 6/13/2026). No outcome was found.
  • In 2025 the Summit County GOP complained about unsigned "GOP Voting Alert" texts in Park City's nonpartisan races (KPCW). No outcome was found.
