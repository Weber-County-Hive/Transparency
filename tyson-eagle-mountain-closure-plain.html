What happened

Tyson Foods is closing its meat plant in Eagle Mountain. 723 people are losing their jobs. The plant opened in 2021. The closing date was Oct. 12, 2026.

Tyson says this is part of a national change in its beef business, tied to a shortage of cattle. It is not about a problem only in Utah.

What Utah offered

In 2019 the state said Tyson could earn up to $5,258,324 in tax credits over ten years. It also offered a separate grant of up to $300,000 for infrastructure.

This was not a check up front. Tyson had to hire people and pay new state taxes first. Then, once a year, it could claim a slice back.

The state says Tyson will not get any more credits once the plant stops running. The part it never claimed was never paid.

How much did Tyson get?

No one has published the exact number. News reports say Tyson claimed between 25% and 50% of its state credit. That works out to about $1.3 million to $2.6 million. That is our estimate, not an official total.

Can the state get money back?

A 2019 state board summary mentions a "recapture" rule. It covers money paid above a 20% cap. It also says Tyson had to commit to stay in Utah for 10 years. The signed contract has not been made public, so we do not know if it lets the state recover money after an early closing.

The city deal

Eagle Mountain also set up a local tax deal for the site, called the Pole Canyon Community Reinvestment Area. A news report in 2019 put a $12.3 million tax break on it. City records say local taxing groups agreed to share 60% of the new property tax growth for twenty years. The city board held public hearings and voted 5–0.

What comes next

The city is looking for a buyer for the building. The land and building can still be taxed while empty. Eagle Mountain will join the new Lake Mountain School District on July 1, 2027. How the closing affects that district's money is still an open question.
