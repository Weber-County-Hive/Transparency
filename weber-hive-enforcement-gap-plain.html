What are these reports?

In Utah, candidates and political groups must file public reports. The reports show who gave them money and where the money went. Officials also file forms that list their business and property ties.

When a report looks wrong, anyone can file a complaint. A complaint is a claim, not proof. A state or county office then looks into it.

Three cases we compared

Stuart Adams, the former Senate president. A group said he listed more than $400,000 in campaign spending as paid to credit-card companies. The reports did not name the businesses that were really paid. A news report said officials found his method did not follow the law. But they applied that only going forward. He was not fined.

Mike Schultz, the House speaker. He gave $120,000 to a political group, called a PAC. The PAC ran ads but reported spending nothing. Complaints went to the Attorney General. About a year later the matter closed with no fine.

Gage Froerer, a Weber County commissioner. He had a small stake in land tied to the Nordic Valley project. He stepped aside from the final votes. A resident complained that he had joined earlier talks. He filed an updated form in November 2022. The county said that met state law. No penalty was found.

Why so few penalties?

Utah does not cap how much a person can give to a state campaign. Big gifts are legal if they are reported. Lawmakers may vote on bills that touch their own jobs. Offices often focus on getting reports fixed. And most cases start only when a reporter or a resident notices.

Penalties do happen. Two past cases ended with resignations, and one with a guilty plea. Those cases involved claims of separate crimes, not just a wrong report.

Is anyone saying a law was broken?

This page does not say that. It shows what the records and news reports say, so you can judge for yourself.

What's new

In August, Adams sent letters threatening to sue four critics. The state auditor said she would not take back her words. As of Oct. 17, 2026, no lawsuit was found in news searches.
