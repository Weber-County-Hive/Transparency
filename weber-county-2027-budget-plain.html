What is a tentative budget?

Every fall, the county writes a plan for how much money it expects to take in next year and how it will spend it. That plan is the tentative budget. Anyone can speak about it at the public hearing on Tuesday, Nov. 24, at 6 p.m., at the Weber Center, 2380 Washington Blvd., Ogden (the commission meeting starts at 5:30 p.m.; county notice). The commissioners vote on the final budget on Dec. 15.

Key dates

From county records and state law.

  • June 23, 2026County Commission adopts the 0.20% sales tax (no ballot question).
  • Oct. 1, 2026The new tax takes effect.

  • Dec. 15, 2026Commission adopts the final 2027 budget.
  • By June 30, 2030 at the latestThe transit share stops being the county's to direct; half then goes to commuter rail.

What can still change?

The tentative budget is a proposal. After the hearing, commissioners can change it before they adopt the final budget on Dec. 15.

The big change: a new sales tax

The county's main account (the General Fund) plans on about $13 million more in taxes in 2027 than the 2026 budget planned. Most of it is the new 0.20% sales tax that started Oct. 1, 2026. It shows up as two new lines worth $9.7 million.

What does the new tax cost you?

It adds 20 cents to a $100 purchase, or $2 to a $1,000 purchase, on things that are taxed. The county's own announcement calls it "1/5 of one cent on a $1 purchase." Of the 0.20%, the county receives 0.15% for public safety and transportation, and cities receive 0.05% for transportation (Weber County, July 1, 2026). Under state law, part of the county's 0.15% is a transit share the county directs only until 2030 at the latest; after that, half of it goes to commuter rail.

Why the county's release says 0.15%. State law (Utah Code 59-12-2220, subsection 5) splits this tax three ways in a county like Weber: 0.10% transit, 0.05% cities and 0.05% county. Subsection 11(c)(i) sends the 0.10% transit share to the county, to use "for a purpose determined by the county legislative body," until three years after at least three counties impose the tax, or June 30, 2030, whichever comes first. So for now the county receives and controls 0.15%. After that, subsection 11(c)(ii) sends half of the transit share to the state's commuter rail account, and the other half stays with the county for transit uses.

Where the new money goes

Almost the same $9.7 million moves right back out of the General Fund: $8 million to other funds and $1.7 million inside the General Fund. The money moved to other funds matches new money for building projects (+$6.5 million) and the ambulance fund (+$1.5 million). The budget book does not say this in words. We matched the totals; the county has not said which dollars pay for which project.

A transfer is the same money moving between county accounts. It is not new spending, so adding the transfer and the receiving fund's spending together would count the same money twice.

The $29 million is the capital plan's total for 2027 and 2028; the $14 million is only the 2027 part, which is inside the $34 million construction line.

Spending savings

Several county funds plan to spend more than they take in next year. The difference comes from money they saved before.

That is not automatically a problem. Savings can pay for a project that happens once, like a building. The bigger concern is using savings every year to cover regular bills. For each fund, the questions are: what is the money buying, how much savings is left, and will next year's income cover next year's regular bills? The budget book's summary page shows how much each fund starts 2027 with and how much it is projected to have left.

RAMP: the tentative budget would use about 87% of RAMP's beginning fund balance ($5.08 million of $5.83 million), leaving $0.75 million. That is a plan, not a result: for 2026 the revised budget planned $11.83 million in RAMP grants, and the county now estimates $7.45 million spent (page 149).

Termination Pool: the budget book says this fund pays employees' leave balances when they leave and health care for retirees, funded by charges to department budgets "based on an estimated percentage of payroll" (page 15). It shows a balance below zero at the start of 2027 (−$1.08 million) and the end (−$1.31 million). The book does not say whether that is an internal accounting arrangement or an amount owed without money set aside. That is a question for the Clerk/Auditor.

Tax money set aside for project areas

In some areas, owners pay their full property tax and the county collects every dollar. Then the agreed share of the growth in those taxes goes to that area's roads, pipes and other projects for a set number of years. The budget book does not add up these commitments in one place. Our full case file lists the ones we found, including Nordic Village and West Weber.

The county's side

No county tax increase in the 2027 budget. Presenting the tentative budget on Oct. 27, the county's budget director said, "No County tax increase is required" (Commission minutes, Oct. 27, 2026).

No property tax increase. The county says the sales tax "does not increase property taxes" and brings in money from shoppers and visitors, not only homeowners (July 1, 2026 release).

The jail plan got smaller. After voters said no in 2023, the jail project dropped from $98 million to about $29 million, keeping the medical and mental health beds and dropping the new sheriff's building.

Savings for one-time projects. Paying for a building from savings is a normal budgeting tool. The General Fund itself is projected to add $1.25 million to its savings in 2027 (page 10).

The law didn't require a vote. State law let the county decide whether to put the sales tax on the ballot.

Questions you could ask on Nov. 24

  1. What does the $9.7 million from the new sales tax pay for?
  2. Which projects does the $8 million moved to other funds pay for?
  3. What pays for the Weber Center remodel and the jail project, since the plan says the money isn't all identified?
  4. What is on the $29.25 million transportation "Special Projects" list?
  5. RAMP plans to use 87% of its savings in one year. What does the extra $5 million pay for, and how will RAMP grants be funded after that?
  6. What will it cost each year to staff and run the two buildings, and what money pays for that?
  7. Will the county post a list of what changed between the tentative and final budgets before the Dec. 15 vote?
