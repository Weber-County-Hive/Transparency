What is this form?

People who hold public jobs fill out a short form each year. It asks what other boards they sit on, what businesses they own and where they work. The point is to let the public see when an official's private ties overlap with their public duties.

What we compared

We looked at the 2026 forms of three people in Wasatch County: County Council member Erik Rowland, Open Lands Board member Dallin Koecher and County Council candidate Rachel Kahler. Then we checked state business records, city contracts and other public records.

Rowland and Kahler checked "no conflict" and left every line blank. Koecher listed one board.

What ties them together

All three are tied to the same downtown nonprofit, the Community Alliance for Main Street, called CAMS. Rowland sits on its board, Koecher is a director and Kahler is its paid executive director. Kahler, Koecher and Matt Brower also sit together on a festival board.

When we checked, the state business registry showed CAMS with a "Delinquent" status. That usually means a yearly renewal was not filed on time. It may have changed since.

Who checks these forms?

Only the official fills out the form. No one is required to check the answers against business records when it is turned in. Questions usually come up only if someone files a complaint.

Is this against the law?

This page does not say it is. It puts the forms next to the public records so you can see the difference for yourself.

What's new

Rowland and Kahler are both on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot. Rowland faces write-in Alex Folmer in Seat C. Kahler faces Democrat Robert McNeill and write-ins Jami Hewlett and Shawn Taylor in Seat A.
