The short version

  • The governor appoints Utah's judges from a list sent by a nominating commission, and the Senate confirms them. Since 2023, the governor appoints all seven members of each commission.
  • Since Oct. 2025, the governor also picks the chief justice, with Senate consent, for one eight-year term. The justices used to choose their own chief.
  • In Jan. 2026 the Legislature grew the Supreme Court from five to seven justices, the Court of Appeals from seven to nine judges, and added three district judges. The courts had asked for eight district judges.
  • Also in 2026, the Legislature created three-judge panels for lawsuits that challenge state laws. The first version let only the state ask for one; a second law a month later let any party ask.
  • Gov. Spencer Cox has now appointed six of the seven justices. As of Nov. 18, 2026, we found no report that he has named a chief justice.

The three-judge panels

H.B. 392 let only the attorney general, the governor or the Legislature ask for a panel. Groups suing the state said that was one-sided. The ACLU of Utah wrote that the law let the state, and only the state, move civil cases it is part of to a panel.

A month later H.B. 366 rewrote the rule. Its text says "a party to a civil action may file a notice" for a panel in a lawsuit against the state or a state official that challenges a law, rule or executive order. Asking costs $1,500. If a court strikes down or blocks the panel law, H.B. 366 creates a separate Constitutional Court instead.

The panels were used right away. In February 2026 the state filed notices to move four pending cases to panels: the redistricting case, the abortion-ban case and two environmental cases. The redistricting case, then before Third District Judge Dianna Gibson, was assigned to a panel on Feb. 26, 2026, according to the League of Women Voters' court filing.

If the panels are struck down: the backup Constitutional Court

What H.B. 366's text sets up, only if a court strikes down or blocks the panel law.

A court strikes down or blocks the three-judge panel law
↓
The Constitutional Court is created: 3 judges, statewide
↓
Its judges are picked through a new 7-member nominating commission, all appointed by the governor
A sitting district or appeals judge can serve without leaving their seat, for an extra 5% of a district judge's pay
Cases already underway can be moved in within 45 days. Once a case is there, a party can't ask to move it back out

In court, the Legislature's lawyer argued that it has authority to set rules for the courts and that nothing in the state constitution bars a three-judge panel. The Utah State Bar said the legislation "subordinates the judiciary," and on Mar. 4, 2026 it warned that last-minute changes to H.B. 366 "threaten judicial stability." Rep. Jordan Teuscher, H.B. 366's primary sponsor, said there can be disagreements in interpreting constitutional issues, and argued for routing those challenges through a three-judge panel (Deseret News, Mar. 4, 2026). The Utah Supreme Court heard arguments on Oct. 5, 2026. As of Nov. 18, 2026, we found no ruling.
