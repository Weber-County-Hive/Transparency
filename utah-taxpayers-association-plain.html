What is it?

The Utah Taxpayers Association is a nonprofit group. News stories often use its numbers on taxes. This fall it supports Amendment B, which would make some citizen tax measures need 60% of the vote.

Most of its money comes from members and donors. The law does not make it share their names.

Who runs it?

We checked its board against the state's list of lobbyists. A lobbyist is someone paid to talk to lawmakers for a company.

Where does the sponsors' money go?

We looked up the political donations of 28 sponsors from 2018 to 2026. Together they gave about $4.3 million to Utah candidates, PACs and parties.

What about Amendment B?

A PAC called Taxpayer Shield backs Amendment B. Its chairman is Sen. Lincoln Fillmore, the bill's primary sponsor. Two Association leaders, Rusty Cannon and Billy Hesterman, are also officers of it.

We found no record of the Association itself paying for Amendment B ads.

Is this against the law?

No. This page does not say anyone broke a rule. Lobbyists can sit on nonprofit boards. Public records exist so you can see these ties and decide what they mean.
